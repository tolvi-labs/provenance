# Tolvi Provenance — Go-Forward Plan

**Status:** Pre-build. Compliance framing dropped; reframed as a durability play. Later probe.
**Last updated:** 2026-07-04

## One-line

A Git-native provenance and durability layer for AI-assisted code: for every change, capture who authored it (human / AI / AI-and-edited), the prompt and reasoning, the context and decisions that shaped it, and a human-review attestation — version-controlled in `.provenance/` alongside the code.

## The correction (why the original framing is wrong)

The marketing site built Provenance's v1 entirely on **EU AI Act Article 50** — machine-readable marking of AI-generated code, an audit-pack for compliance, a public verifier, all tied to the Aug 2, 2026 enforcement date. That premise does not hold:

- Article 50 is a **transparency obligation about user-facing synthetic content** (deepfakes, AI-media, chatbot disclosure), not about marking AI-written source code.
- The Commission's **draft Article 50 guidelines (published 8 May 2026) explicitly exempt source code** at **para. 64** — source code is a "technical output rather than human-facing content," and the exemption covers source code proper including inline comments and docstrings.
- Two further doctrines point the same way: AI code-assistance tools "available only to professional developers" may clear the **obviousness** threshold lifting 50(1) disclosure, and there is a **B2B technical-output exception**.
- The only sliver that stays in scope is standalone natural-language output generated by the same tool — README files, product descriptions, explanatory text — which is an edge, not a product.

So the compliance wedge targets exactly what the regulator has said is out of scope. (Caveat: these are draft guidelines and could shift; not legal advice. But it is specific and repeated enough that a deadline-driven compliance build is not defensible.) See [[2026-07-04-provenance-durability-not-compliance]].

## The reframe: durability, not compliance

Strip the Article 50 mask and Provenance's real value is the **durability story the site itself defers to 2027**: drift detection, mental-model preservation, refactoring confidence — "who wrote this code, why, and on whose authority" for your future self and your team. That is a genuine correctness/context play, on-thesis, and it compounds with the vault as "the audit-grade record under the rest of the stack." Positioning leads with durability; any regulatory angle is limited to the honest in-scope sliver, never sold as Article 50 compliance for source code.

## Priority: later probe, not next build

Provenance is real and on-thesis but **peripheral to the daily build loop** — it records the loop rather than improving it. With no deadline pressure (dates are arbitrary) and no demand pull, it sits behind Forge (ship) and Guild (correctness probe). When taken up, it follows the same evidence-first path: a thin capture-plus-query slice dogfooded on a real product, proving the durability value before any full build.

## Ecosystem role

Provenance is the **record layer beneath the stack**: Tolvi captures decisions, Guild is the execution surface, Provenance captures the authorship and reasoning of each change and cross-references the vault decisions that shaped it. Standalone-capable, Tolvi-native — the "why" sits next to the "who." See [[2026-07-04-guild-correctness-recall-reframe]].

## Immediate cleanup (not a build)

The live marketing copy sells Provenance as Article 50 compliance for AI code and shows those requirements as "shipped." Independent of build priority, that copy should be pulled or reframed onto the durability thesis before the page ever goes public — selling a compliance product to regulated buyers on a misreading of the regulation is a real liability, not just an accuracy nit.
